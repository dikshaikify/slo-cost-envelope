Service blueprint, SLOs and cloud cost envelope

An operational contract for a small two-tier web service (stateless app tier plus one relational database), written before any infrastructure is chosen. Three tiers come from your scenario sheet: Lean (₹2,500/month, 500 users), Balanced (₹8,000, 5,000 users) and Resilient (₹25,000, 25,000 users).

1. Blueprint: request, dependency, deployment, data and failure paths

Paths in words

Request: client, CDN/WAF, load balancer, app node, database, and back. Static assets stop at the CDN. Dependencies: the app hard-depends on the database and the secrets store at start-up only; the CDN, logging and the payment/email APIs (if any) are soft dependencies behind timeouts and circuit breakers. Deployment: pipeline builds an image, pushes to the registry, then rolls one node at a time behind health checks; schema migrations run first and stay backward compatible for one release. Data: one primary database, nightly full backup plus point-in-time recovery on Balanced and Resilient.

Failure paths: F1, an app node dies, so the load balancer ejects it within 2 failed checks. F2, the database is unavailable, so the app returns a clear 503 for writes and cached reads where possible. F3, a bad deploy, so the pipeline halts when error rate exceeds the paging threshold during rollout and rolls back to the previous image. F4, a region or zone is lost, so recovery follows the RTO and RPO in section 2.

2. SLIs, SLOs, error budget and paging

Indicators

Availability SLI: proportion of requests at the load balancer that return a non-5xx response (4xx counts as good; health checks and synthetic probes excluded). Latency SLI: proportion of successful requests completed within the tier's p95 threshold, measured at the load balancer. Both over a rolling 30 days (43,200 minutes).

Paging thresholds (multi-window burn rate)

Burn rate 1 means the budget lasts exactly 30 days. Page on fast burns, ticket on slow ones, so people are woken only when the budget is genuinely at risk.

Latency paging: page when more than 20% of requests exceed the p95 threshold for 15 minutes; ticket at 10% for 1 hour. Budget policy: above 50% of budget spent, only reliability work ships; at 100%, feature releases freeze until the window recovers.

4. Architecture decisions

DecisionChoiceWhy, and what it costs
ADR-1 RegionOne Mumbai-class region for all tiers; Resilient adds a second Indian region for backup copies only.Closest to Bengaluru users, so p95 targets are reachable, and data stays in India. A single region means a regional outage breaches the SLO on Lean and Balanced; accepted.
ADR-2 Recovery objectivesLean: RPO 24 h, RTO 4 h from nightly backup. Balanced: RPO/RTO 60 min with PITR and scripted rebuild. Resilient: RPO 15 min, RTO 30 min via multi-zone failover and tested restore.Targets come from your sheet. A backup counts only after a quarterly restore drill meets the RTO. Resilient's 30-minute RTO covers zone loss, not region loss.
ADR-3 SecretsManaged secrets store; no secrets in images, repos or CI logs; app reads them at start-up via its role; 90-day rotation for database credentials.Removes the most common leak path. Cost: a start-up dependency on the store, so cache secrets in memory and retry with backoff.
ADR-4 Least privilegeSeparate roles for app runtime, deploy pipeline, backup job and humans. App: read its own secrets, connect to its database, write logs. Pipeline: push images and update the service, nothing else. Humans: SSO with MFA, read-only by default, time-limited elevation.Limits blast radius of a leaked key. Cost: more role setup and occasional access requests during incidents; mitigate with a documented break-glass role that alerts on use.
ADR-5 NetworkDatabase in a private subnet reachable only from the app tier; only the load balancer is public.Shrinks attack surface; admin access goes through the audited role, not an open port.
